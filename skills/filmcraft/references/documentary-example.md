# A documentary paper edit

Training packet for a fictional 12-minute community film about a cinema’s last screening. All identifiers below belong to an invented exercise. Use genuine timecoded material for a commissioned documentary.

**Packet** — T1, projectionist interview, 00:08:14–00:08:18, exact words “I kept the light on until the last coat was gone.” V1, observed lobby 21:58–22:04, fixed wide, people leaving. R1, programme for Friday, reads “Final public screening.” V2, daylight exterior on Saturday, lights off. No material establishes who owns the building or what happens to it next.

**Chapter map** — 0:00–0:40 the last coat; 0:40–3:00 how an ordinary shift ends; 3:00–5:30 the announcement; 5:30–8:30 the last screening; 8:30–11:00 who takes what home; 11:00–12:00 the next morning. Chapters 2–5 need interviews or observational coverage. This is a treatment with a scripted opening, not a completed documentary.

| 0–8 s | Room tone, shoe catches on the mat | V1, lobby wide. Hold while the last audience member crosses. | Establish the recorded space. |
| 8–13 s | T1, exact quoted line above, with its original breath | Stay on the lobby. Bring in interview audio before the cut to a programme insert. | The projectionist tells us why the light matters. |
| 13–25 s | VO: “Friday’s programme calls this the final public screening.” | R1, entire programme for context, then isolate those three printed words. | Attribute the claim without inventing a closure date for the building. |
| 25–40 s | VO: “By Saturday morning the lobby was dark.” Let exterior sound carry the handoff. | V2. Label the day change. | The film can now ask how the last Friday differed from an ordinary shift. |

**Pickup** — ask the manager what decision ended public screenings; request a dated record. Until corroborated, cut any line assigning a cause. If V1 has no clean room tone, use recorded matching ambience or silence and log the substitution. Do not manufacture sync sound as proof of an event.
